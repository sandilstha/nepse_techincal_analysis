Entry / exit are selected by the Blend — the per-month average of the realistic return and Opp %. Expected Return % = realistic, bookable price return: outlier-trimmed mean of the window's realised, year-by-year compounded month-over-month move (buy at the accumulate month's close, sell at the exit month's close; dividends excluded). Opp % = a hindsight ceiling — the average best-case intra-month swing over the window, assuming perfect timing of each month's high/low; not repeatable. Blend % = the average of the two, compounded; the table is ranked by it. /Mo % = Expected Return per month held. Confidence blends the real-return hit-rate with sample size and is downgraded when the pattern did not hold in both halves of the history. Windows need 5+ years of history and recompute automatically as new data lands. Timing hints in Recommendation Summary show where the entry month's low and the exit month's high have historically printed (early = first third of the month's sessions, mid = middle, late = final third) — a tendency to refine order placement, not a rule. Note: the window is chosen in-sample from the full history — realised forward returns tend to be more modest than the historical fit.

Avg Return % = average month-over-month change of each index's month-end close, per calendar month across all years. With 6+ years of a month the single best and worst year are dropped before averaging so one extreme year doesn't define the season. Hover a cell to see how many years back it. Best / Worst ranks only months with 5+ years of history. Opportunity % = a hindsight, best-case intra-month swing (not a repeatable return): from the earliest trading day's High down to the month's Low when the high came first, else from that day's Low up to the month's High. It assumes perfect timing of the turn and marks the maximum move the month offered, not one you could have booked in advance. Latest Monthly Return uses the most recent completed month when available; the current partial month is excluded from seasonal averages so it does not skew them. Multi-month gaps in an index's history are skipped rather than counted as a single month's return. Rows follow the Nepali fiscal year — Shrawan (mid-July) through Ashadh (mid-July next year); Nepali labels map each Gregorian month to the Bikram Sambat month that begins within it (approximate).
