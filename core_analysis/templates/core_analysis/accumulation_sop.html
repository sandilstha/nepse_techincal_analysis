A/D Radar SOP

Methodology note for the A/D Radar tab on the Floorsheet Analytics Desk. It reads NEPSE's broker-tagged floorsheet and asks, for every scrip, whether someone is building a position or unloading one. Unlike every other metric on that desk — which are descriptive roll-ups — this one is backtested, so this note documents not only how it is computed but what it was measured to be worth, and where it fails. It is a screening tool, not investment advice and not a forecast.

1. What the tab does

Every other tab on this desk is broker-first: you select a broker and see what it traded. A/D Radar is stock-first: it ranks the whole market by whether each scrip is being accumulated or distributed over a multi-session window.

Two structural facts about how NEPSE campaigns actually run are built into the model rather than assumed:

  • Accumulation is a campaign, not a session. A single day cannot show it, so the window is always multi-session (default 1 month ≈ 22 sessions). The tab will not display a one-day reading.
  • It runs through several brokers, not one. An operator spreads buying to avoid showing a hand. The score therefore penalises buying concentrated in a single broker — see §3.
Everything is computed from trades that already happened. The score describes observed flow; the backtest tells you what that flow has historically been followed by. Neither is a prediction about any individual scrip.

3. The score

For each scrip in the window, every broker's net is computed (its buy quantity minus its sell quantity). Three features are derived from those nets, then each is z-scored across every scored scrip in the same window. The reading is therefore relative to the rest of the market at that moment, never against a fixed threshold that would drift as conditions change.

Definitions, stated exactly

For each broker in the window, net = shares bought − shares sold. Summed over all brokers that is identically zero — every share has a buyer and a seller — so neither ratio below divides by it. Three quantities are needed:

QuantityExactly
sum_posΣ of the positive nets — what the net buyers accumulated
sum_negΣ of |negative nets| — what the net sellers released. Equal to sum_pos by construction (verified exact to floating point on every observation)
volumeGross shares traded in the window = Σ of every broker's buy quantity (identical to the sell-side total). Includes a broker's self-crosses, which cancel out of the nets but not out of this total
FeatureDefinitionSignMeasured effect
absorb(Σ of the top-5 positive nets) ÷ volume — a net numerator over a gross denominator: “what share of everything traded ended up in the five largest net buyers' hands”++57 bps per SD (t = 4.22)
sell_hhiHerfindahl of the |negative nets| as shares of sum_neg, ×10,000. Concentration within the net-selling side only (0 = dispersed … 10,000 = one seller)−−34 bps per SD (t = −5.55)
top1_share(largest single positive net) ÷ sum_pos. Concentration within the net-buying side only−−47 bps per SD (t = −2.23)
score = z(absorb) − z(top1_share) − z(sell_hhi)

The three are weighted equally — and that is a choice

The weights are +1, −1, −1. That is a deliberate robustness decision, not a fitted result. Regression weights on this sample would be roughly (+1.00, −0.87, −0.41) and score better in-sample (+10.88% vs +8.03% at 120 sessions) — but they are fitted to the same 3.5 years they are then measured on. Equal weights cannot overfit the coefficient estimates. What the evidence supports is the sign and ordering of the three features; their exact relative magnitudes it does not, so the score does not assert them.

The two minus signs are the substance of the model, not a detail:

  • Concentrated selling is the most reliable feature measured (largest t-statistic of anything tested).
  • Single-broker dominance scores worse. Conditional on how much is being absorbed, having it funnelled through one broker is a negative: dispersed net buying has historically been followed by better returns than concentrated net buying.
These are statements about flow structure, not intent. The floorsheet records which broker was on each side of each trade. It does not record who the client was or why they traded, so nothing here can distinguish a deliberately-split order from a broker that simply has many retail clients. Where this document says buying was “dispersed” or selling “concentrated”, it means only that — a measured property of the distribution, with no claim about anyone's purpose.

Supporting columns (shown, not scored)

  • Buy Group — the smallest number of net buyers covering half of all net buying. Descriptive: it did not predict on its own, but it is what tells you whether a campaign is broad or one desk.
  • Price Δ — price change over the same window. Absorption without markup is the classic quiet-accumulation pattern; the Quiet Accumulation view filters on exactly this.

4. Universe & filters

The score is only valid on ordinary equities, and the live universe deliberately matches the one the model was validated on.

FilterThresholdWhy
Liquidity floor≥ Rs 500,000 turnover per sessionA scrip that barely trades has no measurable flow structure. Measured in rupees, not shares — see below
DebenturesExcludedBonds — “accumulation” is not a meaningful concept, and they trade through one or two brokers so every concentration measure pins at its extreme
Mutual fundsExcludedClosed-end units; thin, and structurally different from equity
Promoter sharesExcludedTransfer-restricted and locked in — not free float, so the flow is not market flow
Cross-section minimum≥ 12 scored scripsBelow this a z-score is meaningless and the tab reports no data rather than a fake reading
Promoter scrips carry their parent's sector — CZBILP is labelled “Commercial Banks” — so a sector filter alone misses them. They are caught by the security name, which is the only reliable tell: a PO suffix is decisive on its own, but a bare P is not (NADEP, RRHP and SBPP are ordinary equities ending in P).

Why the floor is in rupees, not shares

An earlier version used 800 shares per session plus a Rs 50 minimum price. A share count is not scale-free — the same 800 shares means very different amounts of money:

Share price800 shares/session equals
Rs 200Rs 160,000/day
Rs 1,000Rs 800,000/day
Rs 5,305Rs 4,244,000/day — 26× harder to qualify

That made the bar strictest for expensive stocks, which is precisely where the backtested edge is largest. A turnover floor applies the same economic test at every price level. It also makes the price minimum unnecessary: the Rs 50 line had become vestigial, since the cheapest qualifying equity trades near Rs 190 and non-equity instruments are already excluded by name and sector. Compared in-sample, the two filters perform almost identically (+8.03% versus +7.65% at 120 sessions), so the change was made for consistency rather than for performance — and note that neither figure survives the held-out test in §7.

The sector selector does not change any score

Scoring always runs across the whole market; the sector dropdown filters the resulting list. Ranking inside a single sector would be a different measurement from the one that was backtested — and would make small sectors unusable, since Tradings has 2 listed companies and could never form a cross-section of its own. So a Tradings scrip is shown at its percentile against all ~257 scored equities, not against its one peer.

The count of everything excluded is printed under the table on every scan. This is deliberate: a screen that silently drops a third of the market reads as “nothing is accumulating” when it actually means “we did not look”.

5. Bands & reading the table

Scrips are ranked by score and cut into quintiles — the same buckets the backtest measured, so the band you see is the bucket whose historical performance is quoted.

Neutral is a real answer, not a missing one — it says no side is meaningfully controlling that scrip, which is genuine information. It is styled distinctly so it is never mistaken for a weak buy.

6. The four views

  • Accumulation — the top quintile.
  • Quiet Accumulation — top quintile and price flat or down over the window. These are candidates, not confirmed accumulation: the co-occurrence of heavy net absorption with no mark-up is what the classic pattern looks like, but it is equally consistent with ordinary two-sided trading in a weak tape. The list narrows where to look; it does not establish that a campaign exists.
  • Distribution — the bottom quintile.
  • All Scored — the full ranking, for context on where a specific name sits.

7. The backtest

Method

  • Sample: 2023-01-01 → 2026-08-11 — 819 trading sessions, 9.76 million broker-day-stock cells.
  • No look-ahead: features use only sessions up to the anchor date; forward returns start the day after.
  • Corporate actions: forward returns use bonus/rights-adjusted closes. Raw NEPSE closes book a 10–15% bonus ex-date as a crash, which would poison every return in the study.
  • Market-neutral: results are excess over the cross-sectional median of the same date, so a rising market cannot make a worthless signal look predictive.
  • Independence — corrected. An earlier version of this document reported anchors spaced by the 25-session formation window and called the result "non-overlapping". That was wrong for the longer horizons: two anchors 25 sessions apart with a 120-session forward return share 95 sessions of that return, so the observations were far from independent and the t-statistics were overstated. Every figure below now spaces anchors by at least one full horizon per symbol, so forward windows are genuinely disjoint. Standard errors are also clustered by anchor date to absorb cross-sectional dependence.
Headline finding: the model does not survive out-of-sample. Every feature was originally selected on the same 3.5 years it was then measured on. Holding out 2025 onward — selecting features on 2023–24 only, freezing the score, and testing on data never used for selection — collapses the result. The in-sample figures below are retained only so the size of the decay is visible; they are not evidence of a forward-looking edge.

Retraction. An earlier version of this document stated that dispersed net buying outperforms concentrated net buying (top1_share, t = −2.23). That does not hold. The feature has no univariate predictive power in either sub-period, its in-sample multivariate significance was a suppression effect, and on 2023–24 alone it takes the opposite sign. It remains in the score only because removing it does not improve out-of-sample results either — but no claim should be made from it.

Implementation parity

The research pipeline and the shipped accumulation._scan_market() are separate implementations, so they were compared scrip-by-scrip on three historical windows. Maximum divergence: 0.05 percentage points on absorb and top1_share, 0.5 on sell_hhi — entirely display rounding. What ships computes what was tested.

Survivorship

Observations are dropped when no forward price exists — delisting, suspension, or simply an anchor too close to the end of the sample. Dropping them silently would bias the result if attrition favoured one end of the score, so it is measured:

HorizonDroppedAttrition by score quintile (Q1 → Q5)
20 sessions3.5%3.5% · 3.0% · 3.4% · 3.9% · 3.5%
60 sessions9.5%10.2% · 8.3% · 9.0% · 10.1% · 9.9%
120 sessions18.3%18.6% · 16.8% · 17.4% · 20.0% · 18.7%

Attrition is essentially uniform across the score (18.6% in the bottom quintile vs 18.7% in the top, at the longest horizon), so it is not differentially removing losers from the high-scoring bucket. Most of it is end-of-sample truncation rather than delisting. This is a bias check, not a full survivorship-free reconstruction: scrips that never traded again leave the sample entirely, and NEPSE delistings are rare enough in this window that the residual risk is small but not zero.

One property does hold up: it is not a penny-stock artifact — the in-sample spread is largest in the most expensive price tercile, the opposite of the usual illiquidity trap.

Robustness checks performed

  • Momentum control. Plain price momentum was the strongest single raw feature, so the flow features were double-sorted within momentum terciles. Absorption still paid in all three (+0.50% / +0.61% / +0.72%), so it is not a momentum proxy.
  • Instrument contamination. Re-run on ordinary equities only, in case one-broker bond flow drove the result. It did not — the edge was slightly stronger without them.
  • Window length. Formation windows of 15, 25, 50 and 75 sessions all give substantially the same answer, so the result is not a knife-edge parameter fit.
  • Standard errors are clustered by anchor date throughout.

Notably, the intuitive “the group kept buying day after day” measure did not predict anything. What matters is how much was absorbed and how it was spread, not how many individual sessions it was spread over.

9. Operating steps

  • 1. Pick a window. 1 month is the default; 3 months is a slower, steadier read. A single day is not offered.
  • 2. Start on Quiet Accumulation — absorption without markup is the highest-value list, because the price has not yet moved.
  • 3. Open a row and check the flow detail. Several brokers on the buy side is the pattern that historically scored best; one broker doing nearly all of it is weaker.
  • 4. Cross-check the name on the other desks — Script Deep Dive for the broker split, Stock 360 for fundamentals and technicals. This tab is one input, not a decision.
  • 5. Size for a 3–6 month hold, or do not act on it at all.

10. Limits & blind spots

Predictive power is not established. The in-sample result did not survive a held-out test (+11.15% → +1.64%, p = 0.41). Use this tab as a description of flow structure — who absorbed, how concentrated each side was — which the floorsheet measures directly and which no price/volume screen can see. Do not use it as a forecast, and do not size a position on the strength of the score alone.
Off-market transfers are invisible. Shares moved by DP/BOD transfer, pledge or private deal never print on the floorsheet. A campaign run that way cannot be seen here, and no public data source closes that gap. A Neutral reading means “no on-market campaign detected”, which is not the same as “nothing is happening”.
  • Hit rate ~53%. The edge is an average across many names. Roughly half of top-quintile scrips still underperform, so no single reading should be treated as a call.
  • Regime-dependent. 2025 delivered only +2.7%. It can go quiet for a year.
  • Concentration is not proof of intent. A broker with a large retail client base looks concentrated for entirely innocent reasons. The model measures structure, not motive.
  • Self-crossing dilutes the score. absorb divides by gross traded volume, which includes trades where a broker is on both sides. Those cancel out of the net calculation but still inflate the denominator, so a scrip with heavy self-crossing scores lower than its real absorption warrants. This is left as-is deliberately — the backtest was run on this exact definition, so changing it would ship a rule that was never validated — but it means a heavily-crossed scrip can be under-rated.
  • The price floor is anchored to the window's end, not to today, so historical scans are filtered on what a scrip was worth then rather than now.
  • In-sample construction. Features were selected on the same 3.5 years used to measure them. The robustness checks in §7 reduce that risk but do not eliminate it.
  • Cross-sectional only. Scores are comparable within one scan, not across dates or windows.
  • Not advice. It tells you where to look, not what to buy.
